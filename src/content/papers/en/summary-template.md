A summary is not a translation of the abstract. It is a critical reading that answers one question: **what can I take from this and use?**

Every entry in this section follows the same structure.

## The reference

> **Paper title** — Authors (Year)
> Venue · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org) · [Code](https://github.com)

Always linked. The summary does not replace the original; it helps you decide whether to read it.

## The problem

What was broken or unsolved before this work. Two or three sentences. If you cannot state the problem without jargon, you probably did not understand it.

## The core idea

The contribution, in a single sentence. Then the mechanism: how it works, what makes it different from what came before.

Equations and diagrams go here, but only the ones that carry explanatory weight.

## Results

What they measured, what they compared against, and by how much they improved. With numbers.

| Method | Metric | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | — | — |
| Proposed | — | — |

## Limitations

The section authors write short and we write long. What they did not test, where it breaks, which assumptions may not hold in your case.

**This is the most valuable section of the summary.** A paper with no stated limitations is a warning sign, not a mark of quality.

## What you can use today

The practical part: is there a public implementation? Does it run on accessible hardware? What would you need to adapt?

---

*Rule: if after reading the summary you still do not know whether the paper is useful to you, the summary failed.*
